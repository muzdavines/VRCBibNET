The Branch on His Throne
Text: Zechariah 6:9–15
Focus: Messiah crowned Priest-King

A>Introduction: A Crown on the Wrong Head
We have been following God’s promise through exile.
In Jeremiah 29, God’s people were living in a place they hadn’t chosen.
Babylon appeared to have won. Jerusalem was broken. The temple would fall. Yet God promised that exile would not have the final word.
He knew the plans He had for His people.
Then Isaiah showed us how that hope would be secured.
The Servant would be rejected.
He would be wounded for our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities. The LORD would lay upon Him the guilt of His people.
But death would not have the final word either.
The Servant would be exalted. He would see the fruit of His suffering. He would divide the spoil with the strong.
Now we come to Zechariah.
Some of the exiles have returned to Jerusalem.
But the return has been disappointing.
The city is weak.
The temple is unfinished.
The people are discouraged.
Foreign rulers still control the land.
And the throne of David is empty.
Then God commands Zechariah to perform a strange coronation.
Silver and gold are brought from Babylon. An elaborate crown is made.
But the crown is not placed upon the head of a king.
It is placed upon Joshua the high priest.
A priest wearing a crown.
It looks as though the crown has been placed on the wrong head.
But it is not a mistake. It is a sign.
God is showing His people that another Man is coming.
A King who will also be Priest.
A Priest who will sit upon a throne.
The crown and the altar will meet in Christ.
{Read Zechariah 6:9–15}

1. A Crown Made from Exile
Zechariah 6:9–11: “Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying: ‘Receive the gift from the captives… who have come from Babylon.’”
Three men have arrived from Babylon:
Heldai Tobijah Jedaiah
They bring silver and gold from those still living in exile.
Zechariah is told to go to the house of Josiah, receive their gift and make an elaborate crown.
The materials for the crown have come from Babylon.
The place that represented judgment, captivity and loss is now contributing to a sign of hope.
Babylon ¬ cancelled God’s promise, or destroyed the plan of redemption.
God is taking silver and gold from the place of captivity and using them to announce the coming King.
That connects directly with Jeremiah 29.
God had told the exiles: Babylon is your present address. But it is not your final home.
Now gifts from Babylon arrive in Jerusalem.
The scattered people are beginning to return.
Surprise. “Set it on the head of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest.”
Joshua is not from the royal line. He is a priest.
Israel’s kings came from the line of David. Israel’s priests came from the line of Aaron.
The two offices were kept separate.
The king ruled the people.  The priest represented the people before God.
When kings attempted to take priestly authority for themselves, God judged them. King Uzziah entered the temple to burn incense and was struck with leprosy.
So Zechariah is not simply making Joshua the new king.
¬  permanently Joshua’s crown.
Joshua is becoming a living illustration.
God places the crown on the priest so that everyone watching will ask:
Who is this pointing to?

~Application~
God’s people were looking at everything they had lost.
The throne was empty.
The temple was unfinished.
Their national strength was gone.
But God was still at work.
We often judge God’s faithfulness by what we can presently see.
We see the empty chair.
The unfinished work.
The weak church.
The unanswered prayer.
The person who still seems far from Christ.
And we assume that nothing is happening.
But God can be preparing the sign of His faithfulness while we are still staring at the ruins.
The crown is made from silver and gold carried out of exile.
God has not forgotten His promise.

2. Behold the Man
Jeremiah 23:5 - “I will raise to David a Branch of righteousness; a King shall reign and prosper.”
Z 6:12 ~ Zechariah is told to speak to Joshua: “Thus says the LORD of hosts, saying:
‘Behold, the Man whose name is the BRANCH!’”
Joshua is standing there wearing the crown.
But God does not say: ¬ “Joshua is the Branch.” Joshua is the sign.
The words point beyond him to another Man. “Behold, the Man whose name is the BRANCH.”
The Branch was already part of God’s messianic promise.
Isaiah 11:1- “There shall come forth a Rod from the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots.”
Isaiah spoke of a shoot coming from the stump of Jesse.
Jeremiah promised: “I will raise to David a Branch of righteousness.”
The royal house of David looked like a tree that had been cut down.
There was no Davidic king reigning in Jerusalem.
The people were under foreign rule.
The promise appeared to have been buried beneath the ruins of the city.
But a tree may look dead above the ground while life remains in the roots.
God says that a Branch will grow. The promise has not died.
.         “From His place He shall branch out.”

The Messiah will appear where people see only barrenness.
¬ out of Israel’s political strength, or because the nation has finally repaired itself.
God will bring Him forth. Humanity does not grow its own Saviour.
God gives Him. Centuries later, another ruler would stand before a crowd and say:
“Behold the Man!”

Pilate presented Jesus wearing a crown of thorns. ≈  mockery.
But the words carried more truth than Pilate understood.

Here is the Man. Here is the Branch.
Here is the promised Son of David.

~Application~
God does not need visible strength in order to keep His word.
The throne of David was empty, but the promise to David remained.
The temple was unfinished, but God had already appointed its true Builder.
The people were weak, but the Branch was coming.
Our confidence does not rest in appearances.
It rests in the God who raises life from what appears dead.

3. The Branch Builds the Temple
Zec 6:12–13: “*HE* shall build the temple of the LORD. Yes, *HE* shall build the temple of the LORD.”
The statement is repeated. That repetition matters.
The returned exiles were already rebuilding the temple in Jerusalem.
Joshua served as high priest.
Zerubbabel was governor and oversaw much of the rebuilding work.
But the Branch would build a greater temple.
The stone temple in Jerusalem would eventually be completed.
But it would not solve the deepest problem.
A building could be restored while human hearts remained sinful.
The altar could be rebuilt, but animal sacrifices could not finally remove guilt.
The priesthood could begin operating again, but every priest would eventually die.
God’s people needed more than a repaired building.
They needed a place where God and sinners could truly meet.
Jesus later spoke of His own body as the temple.
“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”
Through His death and resurrection, Jesus becomes the true meeting place between God and humanity.
We do not approach God through a location in Jerusalem.
We come through Christ, the New Testament goes further.
Everyone joined to Christ becomes part of the temple He is building.
Peter says believers are: “Living stones… being built up a spiritual house.” ~ 1 Peter 2:4–5
Paul says the church is: “A dwelling place of God in the Spirit.”
The Branch builds the temple.
He gathers people from different nations, backgrounds and languages.
He joins them to Himself.
He makes them into the place where God dwells by His Spirit.

~Application~
The church is not our construction project.
Christ says: “I will build My church.”
Pastors serve. Teachers teach. Evangelists proclaim.
Believers use their gifts. But Christ is the Builder.
Humbling!! We cannot manufacture spiritual life.
We can create programmes, organise meetings and design attractive spaces. But only Christ can take spiritually dead stones and make them living stones.
Ecouraging!!
The church does not ultimately depend upon our strength.
The same Branch who rose from the apparently dead line of David is still building His people.

4. The Priest Upon the Throne
Zechariah 6:13 ~ This is the centre of the passage.
“He shall bear the glory, and shall sit and rule on His throne; so He shall be a priest on His throne.”

The Branch will bear glory.
He will sit./  rule. / be a priest on His throne.
The crown and the altar meet in one Man.
Normally the king sat upon the throne while the priest stood before the altar.
The king represented God’s rule to the people.
The priest represented the people before God.
The king exercised authority. The priest offered sacrifice and interceded.
But the coming Messiah will hold both offices perfectly.
As King
Christ rules His people.
He commands.
He judges
He protects.
He defeats His enemies.
He establishes righteousness.
His kingdom is not a democracy in which we vote upon His commands.

Jesus is not merely an adviser whose suggestions we consider.

He is Lord.

As Priest
Christ represents His people before God.
He deals with guilt. He offers sacrifice. He intercedes. He brings sinners near.
And unlike every other priest, Jesus does not bring the blood of an animal.
He offers Himself.
Last week Isaiah showed us the sacrifice.
“He was wounded for our transgressions.” “The LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all.” “You make His soul an offering for sin.”
Zechariah now shows us what follows the sacrifice.
The suffering Servant goes to the throne.
The cross was not the defeat of the King.
It was the altar of the Priest. Jesus did not remain in the grave.
He rose. He ascended.
He sat down at the right hand of the Father.
Psalm 110:1, 4 had promised both offices: “The LORD said to my Lord, ‘Sit at My right hand.’”
Then the same Psalm (4) says: “You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.”
The King is also Priest.
And because His sacrifice is complete, He sits down.
The Old Testament priest remained standing because his work was never finished.
Another sacrifice would be required. Another day of atonement would come.
More blood would be offered.
Hebrews 10:11–14 ~ Emphasise that the Old Testament priests stood because their work continued, but Christ offered one sacrifice and sat down.
The Priest is on the throne.
And He remains there.
He rules and He intercedes.
Application
We sometimes separate what God has joined.
Some want Jesus as Priest but not King:
.    They want forgiveness without rule.
.    They want Jesus to remove guilt but not command their lives.

Others think mainly of Christ as King but forget His priestly compassion.
They imagine Him distant, impatient and unwilling to receive weak sinners.
But the King is the Priest.
The One who commands us is the One who gave Himself for us.
The One seated in glory still intercedes for His people.
Submit to His rule. Rest in His sacrifice.

5. The Counsel of Peace
Zechariah 6:13 “And the counsel of peace shall be between them both.”
The wording has produced some discussion.
It may picture peace between the royal and priestly offices.
The simplest point is that what had previously been represented by two separate offices is brought into perfect harmony in the Messiah.
There is no conflict between Christ’s crown and His priesthood.
His justice does not oppose His mercy.
His authority does not cancel His compassion.
His law does not need to be ignored for sinners to be forgiven.
The Priest satisfies the righteous judgment of the King.
At the cross, God does not pretend that sin does not matter.
Sin is judged. The law is upheld.
The debt is paid. And peace is made.
Paul Rom 5:1 says: “Having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.”
Opt. supporting line:
Hebrews 4:16:  “Let us therefore come boldly to the THRONE OF GRACE…”
That is more than a peaceful feeling.
It is possible to feel calm while remaining unreconciled to God.
And it is possible for a believer to feel anxious while still possessing peace with God.
The peace of the gospel is objective.
The hostility has been removed.
The guilt has been answered.
The judgment has fallen upon Christ.
The throne is no longer a place of terror for those who come through the Priest.

Heb 4:16 ~  calls it: “ THRONE OF GRACE…”
Think about that. It is still a throne.
God has not stopped being King.
His holiness has not changed.
His authority has not weakened.
But because our Priest sits there, sinners may approach the throne and receive mercy.
The crown does not bypass the altar.
The altar secures the peace of the kingdom.
Application
We try to serve as our own king and our own priest.
As our own king, we insist upon ruling our lives.
We decide what is right and wrong.
We choose which commands to obey.
We build identity upon our desires.
Then, when guilt comes, we act as our own priest.
We excuse ourselves.
We compare ourselves with others.
We point to good deeds.
We promise to improve.
We attempt to provide our own righteousness.
But we are not qualified for either office.
We cannot rule ourselves into holiness.
We cannot justify ourselves before God.
We need the King to rule us.
And we need the Priest to reconcile us.

6. A Crown Waiting for Its King
Zechariah 6:14–15 ~After the symbolic coronation, the crown is not left permanently upon Joshua’s head.
Verse 14 says it will be kept in the temple: “The elaborate crown shall be for a memorial in the temple of the LORD.”
The crown becomes a reminder.
Joshua will not wear it as though he is now the messianic king.
It remains in the temple, waiting.
Every time the people see it, they are reminded:
A Man is coming. The Branch will appear.
The throne will not remain empty forever.
The true Priest-King will bear the glory.
The crown is present.
But the final coronation is still future.
Then Zechariah says: “Even those from afar shall come and build the temple of the LORD.”
In the immediate setting, this includes exiles still scattered far from Jerusalem.
Others will return.
Others will contribute to the rebuilding.
But the promise reaches beyond the return from Babylon.
The temple built by the Branch will include people who were far away from Israel’s covenants.
People from the nations.
Paul writes to Gentile believers:“You who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.”
Notice how every part of the passage comes together.
The Priest brings them near by His blood.
The King brings them under His rule.
The Builder makes them part of His temple.
Those who were far away are no longer strangers, but become members of God’s household.
This is the greater gathering promised through the prophets.
Jeremiah spoke of God bringing His people home from exile.
Isaiah spoke of the Servant sprinkling many nations.
Zechariah now sees people from afar helping to build the LORD’s temple.
The passage closes:“Then you shall know that the LORD of hosts has sent Me to you. And this shall come to pass if you diligently obey the voice of the LORD your God.”
The promise is not given so that the hearers can remain spectators.
God has spoken. They must listen.
They must continue the work He has placed before them.
They cannot produce the Branch through their obedience. God will send Him.
But genuine faith in God’s promise shows itself by hearing God’s voice.
Application
The gospel is not merely information to admire.
The Priest-King calls for a response.
Hear His voice. Turn from sin. Trust His sacrifice.
Come under His rule.
Take your place among the people He is building.

Conclusion: Crowned with Thorns, Crowned with Glory
John 19:5 “Behold the Man!”
Zechariah saw a crown placed upon the head of a priest.
Centuries later, another crown was placed upon another head.
But that crown was made of thorns.
The soldiers dressed Jesus in purple.
They placed a reed in His hand.
They bowed before Him and mocked: “Hail, King of the Jews!”
Pilate brought Him before the crowd and said: “Behold the Man!” {John 19:5}
The Man beneath the crown of thorns was the Branch.
He was the promised King & the Priest.
And He was about to offer Himself as the sacrifice.
The world saw weakness. God was accomplishing redemption.
The world saw a rejected prisoner. God saw the righteous Servant bearing the sins of many.
The world saw a failed king. But the cross was the altar upon which the Priest-King made peace.
They placed Him in a tomb. But the tomb could not hold Him.
The Branch rose. The Priest completed His sacrifice.
The King ascended to His throne. Jesus now bears the glory.
He sits and rules. He intercedes for His people.
He gathers those who are far away.He builds His temple.
And one day the King who was crowned with thorns will be seen by all creation crowned with glory.
So do not attempt to remain your own king.
Your self-rule has already led you away from God.
And do not attempt to remain your own priest.
Your goodness cannot remove your guilt.
Your promises cannot pay your debt.
Your religion cannot reconcile you to God.
Come to Christ. Submit to the King.
Heb 7:24–25~ “He continues forever… therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him.”

Trust the Priest.
He has offered the sacrifice.
He has made peace by the blood of His cross.
He has risen.
He sits upon the throne.
And because the Priest is upon the throne, everyone who comes through Him may draw near to God in peace.
The crown and the altar meet in Christ.
