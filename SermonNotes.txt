Exile and Judgment
Text: 2 Kings 17
Focus: Exile for covenant unfaithfulness
Christ Focus: Christ bears the exile we deserved and brings His people home.
Introduction
Last week ~ Elijah discover that God was still at work, even when everything looked lost.

Today ~ move several generations later.

The northern kingdom has ignored prophet after prophet.
Elijah, Elisha, Amos, Hosea.

Every warning has been rejected. 

Darkest chapters in Israel's history.
¬  Bc a battle is lost. Bc the covenant appears to fail.
Samaria falls. Israel disappears into exile.
Yet even here, the story is not ultimately about Assyria.
It is about God's faithfulness to His own covenant.
The God who promised blessing also promised judgment.
The exile is not God breaking His word. It is God keeping it.

{2 Kings 17:1–6}


1. The Kingdom Falls
2 Kings 17:5–6::  5 Then the king of Assyria invaded all the land and came to Samaria, and for THREE years he besieged it. 6 In the ninth year of Hoshea, the king of Assyria captured Samaria, and he CARRIED THE ISRAELITES away to Assyria and placed them in Halah, and on the Habor, the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes.

The Assyrians surround Samaria. 3 years the city falls.
The ten northern tribes are carried away.

Politically ≈  end of a nation.
Spiritually ≈ consequence of centuries of rebellion.

Briefly the historian describes the military events. Only a few verses.
Explanation ¬ isn't military. ≈ is theological.
Assyria isn't the main actor. God is.
~Metaphor~
A doctor doesn't cause the disease by announcing the diagnosis.
Assyria is the diagnosis. Israel's sin is the disease.

Matthew Henry: "The ruin of sinners is owing to themselves."

~Application~
We naturally explain events by economics, politics or armies.
Scripture asks a deeper question. "What is God doing?"

{Read 2 Kings 17:7–12}
2. Why Did This Happen?
"This occurred because..." The chapter tells us exactly why.
They feared other gods. Forgot the God who redeemed them from Egypt.
Copied pagan worship Built idols,  Rejected His commands.

Notice the repeated language.
They "did." "They served." "They worshipped."
¬ 1 mistake. It was a settled pattern.
This echoes Deuteronomy.
God had already warned them. Blessing for obedience.
Curse for rebellion.
The exile was never Plan B.
It had always been written into the covenant.

John Calvin: "God's judgments never come without a cause."

~Application~
Sin rarely destroys overnight. It grows slowly.
Compromise becomes habit. Habit becomes identity.
Identity becomes judgment.

2 Kings 17:13–15}
3. God Warned Again and Again
One of the most moving verses: "The LORD warned Israel and Judah by every prophet..."
God did not judge without patience. He sent prophet after prophet.
Elijah. Elisha. Amos Hosea.
Again and again.   "Turn back."
But they would not listen.This is astonishing grace.
Judgment was delayed for generations.
Metaphor
Like a lighthouse flashing through the night.
The rocks don't move.
The warning keeps shining.
Eventually the ship chooses whether to turn.

J. C. Ryle: "God's patience is wonderful, but it is not endless."
~Application~
Every call to repentance is mercy,  Every sermon, Every conviction, Every reading of Scripture, God still calls people to return.

One phrase stands out : "They went after false idols and became false,"
We become like what we worship.
This theme runs throughout Scripture.
Psalm 115::  4 Their idols are silver and gold,  the work of human hands.

    and they do not make a sound in their throat.

!! Says those who make idols become like them.
Lifeless. Blind. Deaf.
The human heart is shaped by its object of worship.
~Metaphor~
Like iron placed near a magnet.
Over time it takes on its character.

Augustine: "We become what we love."
Application
Everyone worships something.
Career. Comfort. Politics.  Identity.Money.
Only Christ transforms us into His likeness.

{2 Kings 17:18–23}
5. Exile Is More Than Geography
The land was God's gift.
Exile meant more than relocation.
It meant removal from covenant blessing.
.   Away from temple. Sacrifice. King.Promise.

The exile pictures what sin always does:  It separates.
It drives us from God's presence.
Adam was exiled from Eden. Israel is exiled from Canaan.
Both point to humanity's deeper problem.
Quote
Sin always takes us further than we intended to go.
Application
Every sinner lives in spiritual exile until reconciled to God.

6. Christ Bears the Exile
The exile doesn't end in 2 Kings.
Even after many Jews return under Ezra and Nehemiah, something still feels unfinished.
The prophets begin speaking about a greater return.
A greater restoration.
Jesus comes as the faithful Israel.
Where Israel failed, He obeys.
Where Israel broke the covenant, He keeps it.
On the cross He experiences exile. "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"
He bears covenant curse.

Galatians 3:13 Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us—for it is written, “Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree”— 14 so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith.

The exile reaches its deepest point at Calvary.
And resurrection begins the true return.
Not merely back to a land.
Back to God Himself.

Sin drove us out. Christ brings us home.

I think it needs to land much harder than "trust Christ because exile is over."

The exile is ultimately a picture of **our exclusion from God's presence**. The gospel isn't simply that Jesus helps us return. It's that **He entered the ultimate exile under God's wrath so that rebels could be brought into the Father's house.**

7. The Greater Exile and the Greater Return
When we reach the end of this part off 2 Kings 17, it feels as though the story has failed.
God promised Abraham a land. Joshua led the people into that land.
David established a kingdom. Solomon built a temple.
Everything seemed to be moving forward.

Now it all appears to be unravelling:  The land is lost. The king is gone. The people are scattered.Has God abandoned His promises? NO!!.

The exile is not the failure of God's covenant.
It is the fulfilment of His covenant.
Back in Deuteronomy, before Israel ever crossed the Jordan, Moses had warned them:
Deut 28:"If you will not obey the voice of the LORD... you shall be plucked off the land... and the LORD will scatter you among all peoples."

God had been faithful in His blessings.His warnings.
The exile proves something that modern people often forget. God is holy.He is patient beyond imagination.


Generation after generation He sent prophets.
He delayed judgment for centuries. But He will not call evil good.
He will not simply overlook rebellion.
If He ignored Israel's covenant-breaking, He would cease to be the righteous Judge of all the earth.

That leaves every one of us with a problem.
Because our hearts aren't fundamentally different from Israel's.
Calvin "the human heart is a perpetual factory of idols."
We smile at Israel carving Baals from wood.
Yet we carve gods from careers. Relationships. Money. Success. Politics.Even our own identity.
We don't bow before statues.We simply worship ourselves in more sophisticated ways.
And if God did not spare covenant Israel, why should we imagine He will ignore our rebellion?

That is why the gospel is such astonishing news.
Jesus came as the true Israel. Where Israel grumbled in the wilderness,
He trusted His Father. Where Israel chased idols,
He loved the Father perfectly. Where Israel broke the covenant,
He fulfilled it completely. Yet when He reached the cross, something extraordinary happened.
The faithful Son received the fate of the unfaithful people.
Notice how His death echoes exile.
He is led outside the city. Rejected by His own people.Cut off from the land of the living.
Then comes that terrible cry: "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?"
That is exile in its deepest sense.
Not merely leaving Jerusalem.or leaving Canaan.
Being abandoned beneath the covenant curse.

Paul explains it in Galatians 3:13 Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us—for it is written, “Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree”— 14 so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith.

Every covenant curse that should have fallen upon us...fell upon Him.
Every judgment deserved by idolaters...fell upon Him.
Every exile earned by our rebellion...fell upon Him.
So that everyone who trusts Him receives the opposite.
Not curse but blessing. Not exile but adoption.
Not condemnation but justification.
Not being cast out… but being welcomed home.
The Bible ends with another great gathering.
John looks and sees: "A great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, tribe, people and language..."
The nations that were scattered because of sin… are gathered because of Christ.
Not around Babel.Not around Jerusalem.Around the Lamb.

That is where history has been heading all along.
So today there are only two ways to stand before God.
Either you stand in your own covenant-breaking...or you stand in Christ's covenant-keeping.
Either you bear your own exile...or Christ bears it for you.
Either you remain outside...or you are welcomed into the Father's house.
There is no third place.No middle ground.
No neutral territory.
The gospel invitation is wonderfully simple.
Stop trusting your idols. Stop trying to justify yourself.
Stop believing you can build a life that reaches heaven.
Come to the One who came down from heaven.Come to the One who bore the curse.
Come to the One who entered exile so that sinners might come home.

Today His arms are open.
Tomorrow His judgment will be final.
So come while mercy is still offered.
Come to Christ.

Conclusion
2 Kings 17 is one of the saddest chapters in Scripture.
An entire kingdom disappears. But it also proves something glorious.
God always keeps His word. He keeps His promises of blessing.
He keeps His warnings of judgment. And because He keeps His word,
we can trust His promise of salvation in Christ. Israel was carried away because of covenant unfaithfulness.
Jesus was carried outside the city because of our covenant unfaithfulness. The exile we deserved fell upon Him.
So that everyone who trusts Him may be brought home.
