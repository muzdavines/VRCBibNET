Elijah and the Still Small Voice
1 Kings 19
Focus: Christ as the Word in weakness
INTRODUCTION

Recap
Last time: David's stained crown and Solomon's shining temple.
David failed. Solomon built.
But neither the king nor the temple was the final answer.
The kingdom divides.
Judah in the south. Israel in the north.
Jeroboam introduces false worship. King follows king. The northern kingdom moves further from God.


Then Ahab arrives.
.       1 Kings 16:30: “Ahab the son of Omri did evil in the sight of the LORD, more than all who were before him.”
Ahab marries Jezebel. Baal worship is promoted. The prophets of the LORD are persecuted.

darkness →  Elijah, prophet of the living God.
1 Kings 18: Elijah prays. Fire falls.
The people cry: “The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!”
Then rain comes.
It looks like the great turning point.
Surely Ahab will repent? Surely Israel will return?
Then…

1 Kings 19.
Fire on the mountain → Fear in the wilderness.
Public victory → Private collapse.
The prophet who stood before hundreds now runs from one woman's threat.
Today's journey:
Exhaustion → The Cave → The Whisper → The Commission
God is not only God on Carmel.
He is God in the wilderness.
Not only the God of fire.
The God who speaks in the whisper.
And ultimately, the Word who comes in weakness.

{Read 1 Kings 19:1–8}
1. WHEN STRENGTH RUNS OUT
Jezebel hears what happened on Carmel.
¬ repentance. A death threat. “By this time tomorrow...” And Elijah runs, 

!surprising!
Elijah confronted Ahab, stood against the prophets of Baal. who prayed and saw fire fall.
Now afraid and running for his life.

He travels south, leaves his servant, goes alone into the wilderness and sits beneath a broom tree.
.       “It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life.”
Elijah is finished, exhausted, afraid, disappointed.
Perhaps Carmel had not produced the result he expected.
The fire fell, the people shouted, the rain returned.
Yet the next morning Ahab is still Ahab. Jezebel is still Jezebel. Elijah is still hunted.
Metaphor
It is possible to climb a mountain and only realise how tired you are when you reach the top.
During the climb: adrenaline, purpose, next step, next obstacle.
Then the pressure drops. suddenly you realise how much the climb has taken from you.
Carmel was the mountain.
Chapter 19 is the exhaustion afterwards.
James 5:17 deliberately reminds us: “Elijah was a man with a nature like ours.”
A great prophet still a man.

Look at God's response.
Elijah sleeps, angel touches him: “Arise and eat.”
Bread,  Water,  Sleep.
Then again: “Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you.”


Notice what God doesn't say: “The journey shouldn't be too great for you.”
God knows Elijah's frame.
Psalm 103:14: “He knows our frame; he remembers that we are dust.”
God's first response ¬ a lecture.
Rest. Food. Water. Strength for the journey.
Application
We sometimes over-spiritualise exhaustion.
Sometimes there is sin to confess. Sometimes correction is needed.
But sometimes the servant is tired.
Parents get tired. Carers get tired. Pastors get tired. People praying for the same person for years get tired.
The journey really can be too great for us.
But not for God.
Grace sometimes looks wonderfully ordinary.
A meal. Sleep. A friend. A quiet day. Scripture arriving at the right moment.
God begins restoring Elijah before Elijah even understands what God is doing.

Transition
Elijah has been fed.
But his thinking still needs to be challenged.
So God takes him back to the mountain.

{Read 1 Kings 19:9–10}
2. WHAT ARE YOU DOING HERE?
Elijah travels forty days and forty nights to Horeb.
Horeb = Sinai. Covenant ground.
Moses met God here. The law was given here. The mountain shook here.
Elijah enters a cave.
Then: “The word of the LORD came to him.”

Elijah may have run. But the Word finds him.
God asks: “What are you doing here, Elijah?”
¬ God lacks information; God is drawing Elijah out.
Elijah's answer: “I have been very jealous for the LORD...”
True.
“Israel has forsaken Your covenant... thrown down Your altars... killed Your prophets...” ~ True.

“And I, even I only, am left.” ~ Not true. Or at least, not the whole truth.
Elijah can see Ahab, Jezebel, Dead prophets, and broken altars.
His own failure and fear.
But he cannot see the seven thousand.
Metaphor
Elijah is looking at the world from the mouth of a cave.
What he sees is real. But it isn't everything.
Discouragement often takes something true and makes it the whole truth.
“The situation is bad.” True.
Therefore: “God is doing nothing.” False.
“I am weak.” True. Therefore: “I am useless.” False.
“I cannot see what God is doing.” True. Therefore: “God is not doing anything.” False.
Elijah needs more than changed circumstances.
He needs his vision corrected by the Word of God.
Application
We count what we can see. Attendance. Responses. Results. Failures. Opposition. Closed doors.
Then quietly assume that what we can count is everything God is doing.
But the kingdom has always involved hidden work.
Seeds underground.
Yeast in dough.
A remnant Elijah cannot see.
God asks Elijah a question.
Then He is going to show him something about how He works.

{Read 1 Kings 19:11–14}
3. THE VOICE IN THE WEAKNESS
“Go out and stand on the mount before the LORD.”
Then comes a great wind ~ So strong it breaks rocks.
“But the LORD was not in the wind.”

Then an earthquake. “But the LORD was not in the earthquake.”

Then fire. “But the LORD was not in the fire.”

Wind → Earthquake → Fire.

These are Sinai images : Power. Majesty. Judgment.
And Elijah knows about fire.He saw it fall on Carmel.
But God is teaching Elijah that His work can’t be reduced to spectacle.

Then:“After the fire the sound of a low whisper.” KJV: “a still small voice.”
Elijah wraps his face in his cloak.
The wind broke rocks.
The whisper brings Elijah to the entrance of the cave.


Important
This passage is sometimes treated as though it means:
“Turn off your mind, sit quietly, and listen for God's private whisper.”
That's not the point.
Elijah is not discovering a technique for personal revelation.

The Word of the LORD is confronting and restoring His servant.

God had spoken through drought.
God had spoken through fire.
Now He speaks quietly.
The power is not in the volume.
The power is in the fact that God has spoken.
Metaphor
A shout can get your attention.A whisper makes you lean closer.
Carmel showed that God is powerful.Horeb shows that He is near.
Historic Voice
Martin Luther often spoke of God working under what appears to be its opposite.
Strength hidden under weakness. Glory hidden under suffering.
Victory hidden under the cross.That pattern reaches its fulfilment in Christ.
Christ as the Word in Weakness
John 1:14: “The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.”
How does God's final Word come? 
Not with an army / Caesar's or  political power.
A child in Bethlehem.A carpenter from Nazareth.

Isaiah had said of the Servant: “He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice, or make it heard in the street.”
Then the cross. Rome looks powerful. The priests look powerful.
The crowd shouts.The disciples scatter.
Christ hangs in apparent weakness.

Yet Paul says: “The weakness of God is stronger than men.”
The world shouts.
God speaks salvation through the crucified Christ.
The still small voice prepares us for a biblical pattern:
God's power is often revealed in ways the world mistakes for weakness.
Application
Don't assume God is absent because nothing dramatic is happening.
Don't measure God's work only by noise.
A quiet conversation may change a life.
A child learning Scripture may bear fruit decades later.
A small church may be deeply faithful.
A prayer may be answered in ways you never see.
A sermon may appear to achieve nothing, while the Word is working beneath the surface.
The question is not: “Was it spectacular?”
The question is:

“Has God spoken?”

And now, in Christ, He has.
Hebrews 1:2: “In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son.”

Transition
Elijah has heard the voice.But God does not leave him in the cave.
The Word that comforts him also sends him.

{Read 1 Kings 19:15–21}
4. YOU ARE NOT ALONE, AND YOU ARE NOT FINISHED
God says: “Go, return on your way.”
Elijah thought his ministry was finished.
God gives him another assignment. Hazael. Jehu. Elisha.
God is still working in history. Kings will rise.Judgment will come.
The prophetic ministry will continue.

`Then God corrects Elijah's greatest misunderstanding:
“Yet I will leave seven thousand in Israel, all the knees that have not bowed to Baal.”

Elijah: “I alone am left.”God: “Seven thousand.”
Elijah's statistics were wrong.
His God wasn't.
Metaphor
A field in winter can look dead. Bare trees. Hard ground. No colour.
But beneath the soil there is life you cannot see.
Spring doesn't create that hidden life.
It reveals it.


God has people Elijah cannot see.
And God has already prepared the next servant.
Elijah finds Elisha ploughing. He throws his mantle over him.
Elisha leaves the oxen and follows.
Elijah thought everything ended with him.
God had already prepared what came next.
Application
This is both humbling and comforting.

Humbling: None of us is indispensable.

Comforting: God's work does not depend on one of us being indispensable.

We are called to be faithful, Not to carry the kingdom on our shoulders.
Parents: faithful.Pastors: faithful. Teachers: faithful. Church members: faithful.
We sow. We water.God gives the growth.

Elijah carries the Word, Elisha will carry the Word.
The prophets continue to carry the Word.

Then John the Baptist comes in the spirit and power of Elijah.
But John says:“I am not the Christ.”

And points beyond himself: “Behold, the Lamb of God.”
The line of prophets points forward.
Elijah bears the Word. Elisha bears the Word. John witnesses to the Word.
But Jesus is the Word.
CONCLUSION
1 Kings 19 begins with a broken servant.
Elijah runs. Collapses. Sleeps. Complains. Feels alone.

Yet God does not abandon him.
He feeds him. Meets him. Speaks to him.Corrects him.Sends him.
And reminds him: You are not alone.You are not finished.

But Elijah is not the hero of the Bible Arc. He is another servant pointing forward.
Elijah stood boldly, then ran in fear. Christ set His face toward Jerusalem.
Elijah sat beneath a tree and asked to die. Christ hung upon a tree and gave His life.
Elijah believed he was alone. Christ truly bore abandonment: “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”
Elijah heard the Word in the still small voice : Jesus is the Word made flesh.
And at the cross the world sees apparent weakness.
A rejected King. A suffering Servant. A crucified Messiah.
But what looks like defeat is victory.
What looks like weakness is the power of God for salvation.
What looks like silence is God's clearest Word: Christ crucified for sinners.
So when you are weak, don't assume God has abandoned you.
When you cannot see what He is doing, don't assume He is doing nothing.
When the world demands spectacle, remember the cross.
When the journey is too great for you, remember it is not too great for Christ.
God met Elijah in his weakness.
And in Jesus Christ, God Himself has entered ours.
The Word became flesh.
The Word suffered.
The Word was crucified.
The Word rose again.
And the Word still calls weary sinners:
“Come to me, all who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”
Amen.
