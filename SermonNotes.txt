The Son of Man
Scripture: Daniel 7
Focus: The Divine Messiah
Introduction — Look Up
Over the last couple of weeks we have been following the promise of God forward.
In Zechariah 6, we saw the promised Branch: the coming King who is also Priest, who builds the temple of the LORD and sits upon His throne.
Then in Ezekiel 36–37, God promised something that no earthly king could produce: a new heart, a new spirit, resurrection life, God Himself putting His Spirit within His people.
Now Daniel 7 pulls the camera back even further.
Who is this King?
What sort of Messiah can reign forever, gather the nations, give life to His people and establish a kingdom that can never be destroyed?
Daniel's answer is staggering.
He sees the Son of Man.
But before Daniel sees the King, he sees the world as it really is.
And it is frightening.
Daniel has lived under powerful rulers. He has seen Nebuchadnezzar dominate nations. He has watched kings boast, threaten and fall.
But in Daniel 7 God pulls back the curtain.
Human empires rise like beasts from a raging sea.
Yet above the sea—
there is already a throne.
And eventually, approaching that throne, Daniel sees someone unlike every beast that came before.
A man. And yet MUCH MORE than a man.

1. The Beasts Rise
Daniel 7:1–8
Daniel sees the four winds of heaven stirring up the great sea, and out of the sea come four enormous beasts.
The first is like a lion with eagle's wings. The second, like a bear, raised up on one side. The third like a leopard, but with four wings and four heads.
Then comes a fourth.
Daniel can barely compare it to anything. Terrifying. Dreadful. Extremely strong.
Iron teeth.

It devours, crushes and tramples.

Later the interpretation is given plainly: “These four great beasts are four kings who shall arise out of the earth.” — Daniel 7:17
The precise historical details, but Daniel's larger point is unmistakable.
This is human empire viewed from heaven.
From below, kingdoms look impressive: armies, monuments, palaces, banners, wealth.
From heaven, human power detached from God becomes beastly.
And there is something deeply biblical about that.
Genesis tells us that humanity was created in the image of God, appointed to rule creation under God.
But rebellion reverses that order.
When human beings refuse to live under God's rule, they do not become more human.
They become less human.
The rulers who were meant to reflect the Creator begin behaving like the creatures they were meant to rule.
Nebuchadnezzar had already discovered this personally. In Daniel 4 the proud king is driven from human society and becomes like a beast.
Daniel 7 scales that picture up to entire kingdoms.

~Application~
We are easily impressed by power.
Political power. Military power. Financial power. Cultural power.
But Daniel says:
Do not confuse power with permanence.
Every [beast eventually disappears | government has an expiry date | empire eventually becomes history}
The question is ¬: Who looks powerful today? Trump, Putin, Tomdaia 
The question is: Who will still be ruling when God opens the books?

2. The Throne Was Already There
Daniel 7:9–12
Then Daniel's attention moves.
.         “As I looked, thrones were placed, and the Ancient of Days took his seat.”
Notice what God does - HE SITS


The sea is raging.
The beasts are roaring.
The little horn is boasting.
And God sits down.
There is no panic in heaven.

The Ancient of Days is clothed in white as snow. His hair is like pure wool. His throne burns with fire. A river of fire flows before Him.
Thousands upon thousands serve Him.
Then: “The court sat in judgment, and the books were opened.”
The beasts make speeches.

_God opens the books._
That is the difference between temporary power and ultimate authority.
The fourth beast appears terrifying until you see who sits above it.

~Picture~
Imagine standing beside a raging sea in a violent storm: Wind. Waves. Darkness.

If the sea is all you can see, everything looks out of control.

Daniel 7 pulls the camera upwards.
The storm is real.
The beasts are real.
Their violence is real.
But above them stands a throne that has never moved.

~Application~
We often judge God's sovereignty from sea level.
We look at politics, war, suffering, cultural change, personal crisis, and ask: “Where is God in this?”

Daniel does not tell us that God will eventually regain control.
He tells us: God never lost it.
The sea may rage beneath Him. The throne remains above it.

3. Then Comes a Man
Daniel 7:13–14
Now we reach the centre of the vision.

.     “I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man…”

Everything Daniel has seen coming from below has been beastly.
Now someone comes who is genuinely human.

** One like a son of man. **
But immediately there is something extraordinary about Him.
He comes: “with the clouds of heaven.”
In the Old Testament, the clouds are repeatedly associated with God Himself.
God rides upon the clouds. God comes in the clouds.
Yet Daniel now sees one like a Son of Man coming with them.
He is human. But He occupies divine territory.
Then: “He came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him.”
And notice the direction.
The Son of Man is not coming from heaven to earth here.
He comes to the Ancient of Days.
He is approaching the throne.
And then: “To him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom…”
Every beast fought for power.
This King receives His kingdom from God.
And His kingdom is completely unlike theirs. “All peoples, nations, and languages should serve him.”
Not one tribe, country or empire. - All peoples.
And the word translated “serve” is particularly striking in Daniel. It is the language repeatedly used for the service rendered to deity.
This is not simply political respect for another successful king.
Daniel sees a human figure receiving universal allegiance.
Then: “His dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed.”
Babylon, persia, greece, salucid, roman, british, 
Every human empire ends.

His does not.


Here is the mystery of the Son of Man.
Truly human,  Distinct from the Ancient of Days.
Yet coming with divine clouds, receiving universal service, possessing an everlasting kingdom.
Daniel sees what the rest of Scripture will increasingly reveal.
The Messiah is not merely another earthly king.
The Messiah is divine.

4. Jesus Takes the Name
Then we move forward several centuries.
Jesus' favourite title for Himself in the Gospels is: “The Son of Man.”

We can hear that and think He is simply emphasising His humanity. But Jesus knew Daniel 7.

And near the end of His earthly ministry, He makes the connection impossible to miss.
Jesus is standing before the Jewish council.

He is the prisoner. They are the judges.
The high priest asks whether He is the Christ.
Jesus answers: “You will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power, and coming with the clouds of heaven.”— Mark 14:62
Jesus brings together Daniel 7 and Psalm 110.
The Son of Man. The clouds of heaven. The right hand of God.
And the high priest tears his robes. Why?
Because he understands what Jesus is claiming.
Jesus is not merely saying: “I am a man.”
He is saying: “I am Daniel's Son of Man.”
The One who comes with the clouds. The One who receives the everlasting kingdom.
The One before whom the nations will bow.
And there is an extraordinary reversal here.
Jesus appears to be standing before their court.
But He tells them that one day—
they will stand before His.

5. The Son of Man Goes to the Cross
But this is where the story becomes surprising.
If Jesus is Daniel's Son of Man, we might expect Him immediately to destroy the beasts.
Instead- the beasts seem to destroy Him.

Rome [arrests Him | beats Him | strips Him | nails Him to a cross.]
And Jesus allows it.

Because the Son of Man had already explained why He came:
.       “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.” - Mark 10:45

Daniel tells us the Son of Man will receive universal service.
Jesus tells us that before He receives that service- He first comes to serve.

The Divine King kneels. The Lord of glory becomes the suffering servant.
The Judge of Daniel 7 allows Himself to be judged.

WHY?

Because our biggest problem is not the beasts out there.
It is the rebellion in here.
We have all tried to establish our own little kingdoms.
We want God's world without God's rule.
We want to decide what is good, what is true, what our lives mean, who we will be.
The beastly kingdoms of Daniel are simply human rebellion written large.
And God will judge it.
The books will be opened.
That should frighten us far more than the beasts.
Because one day our lives stand before God too.
But this is the gospel: The Son of Man who will one day sit in judgment first came to bear judgment.
At the cross Jesus stood in the place of sinners.
Our guilt.
Our rebellion.
Our self-rule.
Our judgment.
Laid upon Him.
The King wears a crown of thorns.
The Judge is condemned.
The Son of Man gives His life as a ransom.

6. The Son of Man Receives His Kingdom
And then Rome does the worst thing it can do.
It kills Him.  And three days later-

HE GETS UP!!!.

The beast can kill the body. It cannot defeat the Son of Man.

Jesus rises from the dead. And then He ascends.
Now Daniel 7 comes sharply back into focus.
Daniel saw the Son of Man coming to the Ancient of Days.
•    Jesus rises.
•    Jesus ascends.
•    Jesus is exalted to the right hand of the Father.

Peter announces at Pentecost:
“God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you crucified.”
— Acts 2:36
The Son of Man has approached the throne.
The kingdom has been given to Him.
So Christianity is not announcing:
“One day Jesus might become King.”
We are announcing:
Jesus Christ is King.
Already.
Now.
The world may refuse His rule. Governments may ignore Him.Cultures may mock Him.
Individuals may reject Him.
But their rejection does not constitute a veto.

Daniel has already seen the end of the story. “His dominion is an everlasting dominion.”

We are not waiting for Jesus to become King.
We are waiting for the King to return.

7. The Kingdom of the Son Becomes the Kingdom of the Saints
Daniel 7:15–27
Daniel himself is troubled by what he has seen.
And the interpretation acknowledges something important.
The saints will suffer.
The beastly kingdoms wage war against God's people.
For a time, it can even look as though the beasts are winning.
V 21: “This horn made war with the saints and prevailed over them…”
Two words then change everything:
“until the Ancient of Days came.”
There is an until on every beastly kingdom.
Their power has a boundary.
Their persecution has a boundary.
Their boasting has a boundary.
Then Daniel is told: “The saints of the Most High shall receive the kingdom and possess the kingdom forever.”

The Son of Man receives the kingdom.
And His people receive the kingdom with Him.
The King shares His inheritance with those who belong to Him.
That is the security of the Christian.
Our hope is not that we will somehow become stronger than every beast.
Our hope is that we belong to the One who already conquered them.

Conclusion — Look Above the Beasts
Daniel 7 gives us two ways of looking at reality.
Look horizontally and you see beasts.
•    Kingdoms rise.
•    Governments boast.
•    Cultures change.
•    Christians suffer.
•    Lives shake.
The sea can look completely out of control.
But Daniel says:
Look up.
There is a throne.
And approaching that throne is the Son of Man.
Zechariah showed us the Branch upon His throne.
Ezekiel showed us the God who gives new hearts and resurrection life.
Daniel shows us who stands at the centre of it all.
Jesus Christ.
The truly human King.
The Divine Messiah.
The Son of Man who came with divine authority.
The Son of Man who stooped to serve.
The Son of Man who gave His life as a ransom.
The Son of Man who rose from the grave.
The Son of Man who ascended to the Father.
The Son of Man who reigns now.
And the Son of Man who will come again.
One day the books will be opened.
Every human kingdom will end.
Every boast will stop.
Every knee will bow.
And that means the question Daniel 7 finally puts before us is intensely personal.
Whose kingdom are you living for?
You can keep trying to rule your own life.
You can define good and evil for yourself.
You can build your identity, defend your little kingdom and live as though the throne were empty.
But it isn't.
The King has come.
And the King offers mercy before He brings judgment.
So do not wait until the books are opened to discover who sits on the throne.
Turn from your rebellion.
Stop trusting your goodness, your religion, your morality or your ability to repair yourself.
Come to Jesus Christ.
The Judge is the One who offered Himself for sinners.
The King has paid the ransom.
The Son of Man has risen.

Trust Him.

Bow to Him now—not merely because one day every knee must bow—
but because this King is worthy.
And Christian, when the world looks increasingly beastly:
do not panic with the sea.
Look above it.
The beasts have their moment.
The Son of Man has the kingdom.
And His kingdom will never pass away.

Prayer
Thank You, Father, that Your throne is never shaken by the kingdoms of this world. Forgive us when we fear human power more than we trust You. Thank You for sending the Son of Man, our Divine King, who gave His life as a ransom for sinners and rose in victory. Give us grace to submit gladly to Christ's rule, confidence to live as citizens of His everlasting kingdom, and boldness to proclaim Him while mercy is still offered. Come, Lord Jesus. Amen.
